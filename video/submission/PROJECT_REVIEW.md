# Submission review and priorities

## The strongest story

Show one owner getting from a real-world question to a useful handoff. The visual peak is the returned SAM mask and close-up; the product payoff is the editable workshop request and persistent planned record. The archive gives the product character, but a tour through seven navigation items would consume the film without proving the workflow.

Use the mirror as the main case. Use the front tyre as a short second case showing that the workflow can also stop at an appropriate physical check. The 3D study gets a short, purposeful cut: where this component sits, how the assembly is arranged, then back to the action.

## Product changes made for this journey

- Agent inspection is the primary path, visible alongside the photo.
- The existing overview is reused; it is not requested a second time.
- The owner concern refines the focus to mirror or front tyre when that assembly is visible.
- Actual returned SAM masks appear while the agent is working; the real crop is shown during close-up analysis.
- A single acceptance advances to the component workspace. Manual choice remains available as a recovery route.
- Accepted component labels remain consistent in the request and garage record.
- Failed close-up calls are distinguished from a successful call with no extra finding.
- The recording stage frames the live app and shows actual pointer movement/click rings.

## Remaining weaknesses, in order

1. **Vehicle identity and source applicability.** The generated fixture received a Primavera guess. GTS/125 is owner-selected reference context, not model verification. Exact year/market and catalogue applicability still need review. Do not demonstrate automatic fitment or an exact replacement order.
2. **Evidence is prepared, not live retrieval.** The agent handles vision, focus, segmentation and close-up analysis. The next-action guidance is bounded authored content with manual locators. There is no demonstrated autonomous manual search, supplier comparison or external booking.
3. **Request generation is a structured handoff.** The current draft is useful, but it is not a repair diagnosis or a price quote. Show the editable text and its unknowns. Sending remains a separate user action.
4. **Visual fidelity.** The front assembly is a credible supporting study at a reasonable camera distance. It is not the visual centrepiece for 30 seconds and should not be marketed as factory-accurate. Keep its illustrative label visible.
5. **Coverage and reliability.** Two successful fixtures are a rehearsal, not an evaluation benchmark. Keep the failed SAM take and add varied real owner photos before making reliability claims.
6. **Garage continuity.** The planned decision persists, but the record does not yet preserve a complete attachment/evidence dossier or a verified parts history. Storage remains local. Unknown mileage currently defaults to zero, so use an explicitly demo-labelled scooter.

## Final capture priorities

Use a stable server and one browser profile. Record one uncut successful mirror run, then short purposeful takes for the 3D, request, saved passport and tyre check. Cut waiting time openly. Keep text legible and give each decisive interaction a pause. A calm neutral browser frame is enough; dramatic transitions and extra interface overlays would compete with the actual mask and result.

The narrated still preview is an editing reference, not a submission-ready screen recording. Replace its stills with the real clips before export. Keep the 175-second budget and make the ending about the completed owner task.
